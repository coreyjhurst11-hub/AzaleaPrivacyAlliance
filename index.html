THE PROBLEM

Surveillance without oversight.

Across Georgia and the country, law enforcement agencies have installed networks of AI-powered cameras — often known by the brand name "Flock" — on public streets. These Automated License Plate Readers (ALPRs) don't just watch for suspected criminals. They record every vehicle that passes: make, model, color, license plate, bumper stickers, and the exact time and location — regardless of whether the driver is suspected of anything at all.

This data doesn't stay local. It feeds into a searchable, centralized network that law enforcement agencies across the country can access.

WHY IT MATTERS

Abuse isn't hypothetical — it's documented.

Because this system operates with little regulation and limited oversight, it has already been misused. Officers in multiple states — including here in Georgia — have been accused or convicted of using these databases to illegally track romantic partners, family members, and other private individuals who were never suspected of any crime.

When a surveillance system this powerful operates without meaningful checks, abuse isn't a hypothetical risk — it's a documented pattern.

WHAT WE'RE ASKING FOR

Not removal. Accountability.

The Azalea Privacy Alliance is not asking for these systems to disappear overnight. We recognize that ALPR technology is already deployed across much of the state. What we're demanding is accountability.

  • TransparencyClear public disclosure of how camera data is collected, stored, shared, and used.
  • Access controlsStrict limits on who can query the database, and for what purposes.
  • Audit trailsMandatory logging and regular review of every search performed.
  • AccountabilityReal consequences for officers who misuse the system — including access used to track romantic partners, family members, or acquaintances.
  • Retention limitsClear rules on how long data is kept and when it must be deleted.
